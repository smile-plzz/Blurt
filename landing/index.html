The whole loop, four beats

Nothing to file, nothing to rank, nothing to schedule. You speak; it handles the rest and asks you one thing at a time.

  1. One

    You say it

    Open Blurt, speak, close it. It confirms with one line and fades. No confirmation screen, no dismiss button, no decision to make while the next thought is already leaving.

  2. Two

    It sits in the feed

    A plain list of the things you said. Items with something to report carry a small tag. Everything else carries nothing at all — no tag is the resting state.

  3. Three

    It comes back, worded for you

    Sometimes plainly: “Open the lecture.” Sometimes as a question: “Still on your mind, or can this go?” It picks based on what you said during setup and how you've answered before, and it will tell you why it asked when it did.

  4. Four

    You answer, or you let it go

    Done, not yet, did it another way, or let it go. All four are real endings. Nothing is left hanging over you for having chosen one of them.

What makes it not a to-do app

To-do apps assume you can already break things down, rank them, and stick to a plan. That assumption is the thing that's broken.

  • Capture takes one tap and no typing

    No list to pick, no category, no due-date prompt. Speak, done.

  • Letting something go is a real outcome

    “Doesn't matter anymore” is a button, not a failure. It gets recorded as a legitimate ending, same as finishing.

  • No streaks, no counts, no overdue badges, no red

    Deliberately. Those are the exact mechanics that turn an app into a source of guilt by day four, and guilt is what makes people delete these things.

  • Disappear for two weeks and it asks one question

    Not a wall of everything you missed. It asks what's on your mind right now, and only then brings up one old item.

Where your words go

Everything you say is stored in your browser, on your device. No accounts, no sync, no analytics of any kind. Two things do leave the device, and the app says so on its own settings screen:

  • Speech-to-text

    In Chrome and Edge, the browser's built-in speech recognition runs on your browser vendor's servers. Typing instead keeps everything local.

  • The wording of a check-in

    An AI model writes how each check-in is phrased. It receives the text of that one item and a small slice of your setup answers — never the audio, never your full history. Without it, check-ins fall back to fixed wording and nothing breaks.

The app's own settings screen states this in the same plain terms, including what is deliberately not collected.

Where the project actually is

A working prototype, self-tested by one person. Capture, the feed, check-ins, the model that picks tone, breaking a big thing into steps and recovery mode all work end to end.

The tone and the timing have not been tested on anyone but the author. Whether a check-in lands as helpful or as nagging is exactly the part one person cannot answer alone — which is why it is public, and why the useful thing you can do is use it for a few days and say where it got it wrong.
