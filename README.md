# Blurt

**Say the thing before you lose it.**

You think "I should reply to that email" — and it's gone. Not forgotten because you didn't care. Gone because the thought had about four seconds of life in it and nothing caught it.

Blurt catches it. Open it, say the thing out loud, close it. That's the whole interaction. Later, it brings the thing back — not at a rigid due date, but at a moment and in a tone that fits how you actually work.

It is built for ADHD brains, by someone with one.

## What makes it not a to-do app

To-do apps assume you can already break things down, rank them, and stick to a plan. That assumption is the thing that's broken. So Blurt does it differently:

- **Capture takes one tap and no typing.** No lists to pick, no categories, no due date prompt. Speak, done.
- **Letting something go is a real outcome.** "Doesn't matter anymore" is a button, not a failure. It's recorded as a legitimate ending, same as finishing.
- **There are no streaks, no counts, no overdue badges, no red.** Deliberately. Those are the exact mechanics that turn an app into a source of guilt on day four, and guilt is what makes people delete these things.
- **When you disappear for two weeks and come back, it doesn't greet you with a wall of everything you missed.** It asks one question: what's on your mind right now.
- **It decides how to ask.** Sometimes plainly ("Open the lecture."), sometimes as a question ("Still on your mind, or can this go?"), based on what you told it during setup and how you've responded before.

## Where the project is

A working prototype, self-tested by one person. Honest status: capture, the feed, check-ins, the AI that picks tone, breaking a big thing into steps, and recovery mode all work end to end. The tone and timing have not yet been tested on anyone but the author — which is exactly the part that needs real users, and the reason this is public.

Everything is stored in your browser, on your device. No accounts, no sync, no analytics of any kind. Two things do leave the device and the app says so in its own settings screen: speech-to-text runs on your browser vendor's servers in Chrome and Edge, and the wording of each check-in is generated by an AI model. Full detail, including what is deliberately *not* collected, is in [`PRIVACY.md`](PRIVACY.md).

## Try it locally

No build step, no dependencies:

```bash
npx serve .
```

Then open `/frontend/web/` in Chrome or Edge. Voice capture uses the browser's built-in `SpeechRecognition`, which Safari and Firefox don't fully support — typing works everywhere.

### Optional: the AI check-ins

Blurt works without an API key — check-ins fall back to fixed wording and nothing breaks. To enable the AI that picks tone and breaks tasks into steps, set `MISTRAL_API_KEY` in your deployment's environment variables (on Vercel: Project → Settings → Environment Variables). The serverless functions in [`api/`](api/) read it; it is never sent to the browser.

Append `?dev=1` to any app URL to show the build instruments — the manual tone picker and a readout of which decision path ran.
