Onboarding spec

Ten questions, three passes

A context profile, not a screening test. The sequence moves from where you are right now to how things go wrong to how you want to be helped — so early answers are easy and low-stakes, and the questions that shape tone come last, once the user has warmed up.

Each card below lists the question, its answer set, the signal it writes to the persona, and what that signal changes downstream. Field names are proposals for schema/persona.json.

Pass 1 — Where you are right now

Q1–Q3 · lowest stakes, answered in ~20s

Present state before history. These three are answerable without any self-reflection, which is what makes the harder ones later feel safe.

Q1

How's your head right now?

Pretty full — a lot going on
Foggy — can't get a grip on anything
Flat — nothing feels worth starting
Fine, mostly — here to get ahead of it

Signal: entry state → entry_state. Separates crisis-arrival from calm-arrival.

Changes: "pretty full" or "foggy" skips the rest of onboarding after Q3 and drops straight into Recovery Mode with a shorter question set; onboarding resumes later. Nobody in overwhelm should be made to finish a form.

Q2

What's a thing you've been meaning to do?

Small and annoying (a call, a form, a chore)
Big and vague (a project, a move, a change)
Something for someone else
Honestly, a pile of all three

Signal: dominant intent shape → typical_intent_class.

Changes: "big and vague" pre-arms the decomposition trigger (Section 6) — that user's first captures are far more likely to be multi-step. "Small and annoying" biases toward activation-only framing, where naming the whole task adds nothing.

Q3

When do you usually notice you meant to do something?

Right as I'm falling asleep
Mid-task, about something unrelated
When someone reminds me
Too late to do anything about it

Signal: capture windows → intent_surface_moments[]. Also a soft read on moment C (started, got distracted) vs. moment F (disappeared).

Changes: seeds candidate check-in windows before any behavioral history exists. "Mid-task" is the strongest signal for keeping capture friction near zero — that user needs the widget, not the app.

Pass 2 — Where things come apart

Q4–Q7 · the differentiating four

These carry the most personalization weight. Q4 and Q5 are the two that most separate users from each other; if onboarding has to be cut short, keep these.

Q4

highest value

Where do things usually stall for you?

Starting — I know what it is, I just can't begin
Deciding — I don't know which thing to do first
Finishing — I start plenty, I just drift off
Remembering — it's gone before I can act

Signal: primary failure point → primary_stall_point. Maps directly onto moments B / A / C / F in Section 3.1.

Changes: the single biggest prior on moment detection, which the ambiguity table says is the hard part. "Starting" → default activation-only (smallest next physical action). "Deciding" → decomposition offered earlier and the rollup view surfaces one item at a time. "Finishing" → check-ins ask about the in-progress thing before offering anything new. "Remembering" → optimize capture, resurface sooner.

Q5

highest value

When you've been avoiding something, what's usually behind it?

It's boring, and I can't make myself care
It's bigger than it looks and I don't know where to start
I'm dreading it — something about it stings
I keep thinking there'll be a better moment

Signal: avoidance mechanism → avoidance_driver. The why behind a stall, which Q4 alone doesn't give.

Changes: decides what a repeat stall means. Dread → never escalate frequency; switch to inquiring early and treat dropped as a genuinely good outcome. Size → propose decomposition instead of re-reminding. Boredom → activation-only, tiny first action. Timing → the same task at a different window, same framing.

Q6

When in the day do you actually have something in the tank?

Early — mornings are my good hours
Late — I come alive at night
In bursts, unpredictably
Rarely — most days are a slog right now

Signal: usable-energy windows → energy_windows, feeding the receptivity input in Section 3.3.

Changes: when check-ins fire, and how demanding they're allowed to be. "In bursts" means never schedule by clock — wait for an app-open. "Rarely" caps intervention volume from day one and makes silent-recovery the default rather than a fallback.

Q7

How long do you usually go before checking in on your own list?

Most days
Every few days
Every couple of weeks, if that
I don't keep one — that's the problem

Signal: personal gap baseline → gap_baseline_days. This is the question Section 3.4 asks for by name.

Changes: the Recovery Mode trigger threshold, before two weeks of history exist. Without it, recovery either fires constantly at light users or never fires at all.

Pass 3 — How you want to be met

Q8–Q10 · tone and intent

Last, because they ask the user to picture being interrupted — an easier question once they've already described how things go wrong.

Q8

highest value

A reminder lands and you haven't done the thing. What helps?

Just say the thing. "Open the lecture."
Ask me. "Still on your mind, or can this go?"
Give me the first inch, not the whole task
Leave me alone and bring it up next time I'm here

Signal: starting framing mode → preferred_framing. The four options are exactly the four modes in Section 3.3, written as the user would say them.

Changes: which template step 2 hand-triggers, and later the prior the inference layer starts from instead of guessing. Also the honest answer to "stop nagging" — option four selects silent-recovery as the default channel, no notifications at all.

Q9

Where does it fall apart most?

Work or study things
Home and admin — bills, forms, appointments
People — replies, plans, birthdays
Looking after myself
All of it, fairly evenly

Signal: drop-prone categories → drop_prone_domains[], the cold-start value for inferred_patterns.task_categories_prone_to_drop.

Changes: urgency tiering and which stalled items Recovery Mode picks. Also the one signal that lets tone shift by domain — a missed self-care intent should never be framed like a missed deadline.

Q10

What would make this worth keeping on your phone?

Catching things before I lose them
Actually finishing a few of them
Feeling less behind than I do now
Getting one specific thing off my back

Signal: success definition → stated_goal.

Changes: which metric this user's experience optimizes for, per Section 3.5. "Less behind" makes a rising dropped count something to affirm out loud rather than quietly log; "one specific thing" hands the very next capture straight into the decomposition path.

Keeping it short

Ten cards is the full set, not the required path. Q1 can end onboarding early. Q4, Q5 and Q8 are the three that carry most of the personalization weight — if a user bails at any point, the persona is still usable with those alone. Q7's answer is the only one that can't be inferred from behavior later, so it should never be the question that gets cut.

One screen, one question

No progress percentage and no "question 4 of 10" counter — a visible remaining count is the same pressure mechanic as an overdue badge. A quiet segmented progress bar (as in mockup screens 02-03) reads as position, not debt. Every question is skippable with a plain "skip" rather than a "prefer not to say."

Where the voice prompt sits

The existing open voice prompt ("tell me about a time it slipped") comes after Q10, framed as optional. By then the user has answered ten concrete questions and knows what the app is for, so the open answer tends to be specific rather than a shrug. Its transcript enriches the same fields the MC answers seeded, and never overwrites them.

Not a diagnosis

No question asks about symptoms, subtype, medication or duration. Subtype, if it's still wanted, is better inferred from Q4 and Q6 than self-reported — and nothing downstream currently needs it. The wording throughout describes situations, not traits.
